# RAND-based Defence family survey — analysis contract

This contract describes how a future authorised collection of the current adult Appendix A adaptation would be interpreted. **The GitHub Pages presentation has no receiver and contains no collected survey responses.** The adult instrument is version `rand-appendix-a-greater-darwin/1`; child and youth routes are separate local forms. [RAND Appendix A](https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/monographs/2011/RAND_MG1124.pdf) is a source instrument, not a validated Greater Darwin prevalence scale.

| Measure | Unit and interpretation |
|---|---|
| Q12–Q20 problem choice | One respondent selected one specific source-derived difficulty. A check does not establish a request for help or an unmet need. Each domain's explicit “none” differs from a blank or an unasked domain. |
| Q22 priority | A category chosen as one of the two most significant when more than two categories were reported. One or two reported categories can carry forward without an explicit comparative ranking. |
| Q23/Q24 need | A help type the respondent says they needed for one priority problem category. “No need for assistance” is a distinct, exclusive answer. |
| Q25 priority need | One of up to two needs explicitly chosen for deeper follow-up when a category had more than two. A skipped priority is not an inferred selection. |
| Q26–Q29 contact | A military, nonmilitary or personal resource contacted to try to meet **one particular problem–need pair**. No contact is explicit; blank is unknown. Contact is not proof of service receipt. |
| Q30–Q33 resource characteristic | One statement about one resource category across the followed-through needs, not a separate barrier for every problem–need pair. Positive and negative statements may coexist. |
| Q34 personal network | A separately reported statement about friends and family, eligible only when personal networks were contacted. |
| Q35 helpfulness | One rating of one contacted resource for one problem–need pair. Do not convert it into an all-sources overall need-met rating. |
| Q36 loss impact | A hypothetical judgement about losing access to a resource, not proof of past use or unmet need. |
| Q37–Q61 background | Context variables, including deployment and housing, are reported at their stated reference point. Australian rank is not a US pay-grade equivalent; Q49/Q50 are new local cultural-demographic constructs, not comparable US codes. |
| Q62–Q66 attitudes | Respondent views or perceptions of another person's views about military service; they do not prove actual retention or readiness. |
| Q67 text | Additional comments that may cover problems omitted by fixed questions or not chosen for deep follow-up. |

Report the question-specific eligible and answered denominator, wording revision, collection dates/stage, adult versus child route and missingness. A voluntary or convenience sample describes its participants; it does not establish prevalence among all Greater Darwin Defence families. Several members of one household may contribute separate perspectives. Do not equate one person, one selected category, one resource contact and one problem–need pair.

If real fieldwork is authorised, freeze exact option order and branch logic, preserve raw answers before coding, avoid small-cell identifying combinations, and keep consultation contact details in a separate restricted system. Interpret interviews alongside survey findings without pooling spontaneous interview mentions with prompted checkbox counts. The historical fictional `results.html` is not a result view for this instrument.
