# Participation, privacy and contact-form review

Reviewed against public official sources on 25 September 2026. Scope: Lutheran Care's NT Defence Family Support Program **service consultation**, its questionnaire and its separate request for a conversation. Contact-form implementation references were updated on 27 September to match the current binary-age request design; that update is not a new legal assessment and does not change the main survey’s age/permission routes. This review is not a certification of an unimplemented receiving system.

## Practical conclusion

The consultation can include children. There is no general rule banning an under-15 child from completing a contact form. The current request form uses Under 18 / 18 or older for a self-request and infers under-18 status for a guardian’s request. It permits a brief general topic and scheduling preferences; LC establishes the young person’s understanding, appropriate permission and willingness before the actual interview. Selecting an age group does not make that assessment.

The participant notices cover the main purposes, voluntary participation, access restrictions, intended reporting, safety exceptions and privacy contacts. They should remain short and visible. Distinguish the survey from the identifiable contact request, explain guardian requests, and keep request topics brief and non-sensitive; a request does not authorise a detailed interview or recording.

## Eighteen and fifteen mean different things

**Eighteen is the age of majority in the NT.** Section 4 of the [Age of Majority Act 1974](https://legislation.nt.gov.au/api/sitecore/Act/PDF?id=11583) provides full age at 18. The current [NT legislation entry](https://legislation.nt.gov.au/en/Legislation/AGE-OF-MAJORITY-ACT-1974) marks the Act in force. The Australian Law Reform Commission also describes 18 as legal adulthood nationally in [Report 84, paragraph 1.21](https://www.alrc.gov.au/publication/seen-and-heard-priority-for-children-in-the-legal-process-alrc-report-84/1-introduction-3/the-report-its-scope-and-its-context/); that report is historical, while the NT conclusion is checked against current legislation.

**Fifteen is an OAIC capacity presumption, not adulthood.** The Privacy Act does not establish a fixed age for all privacy decisions. A child may understand a particular decision before adulthood. Where individual assessment is impracticable or unreasonable, the OAIC permits an organisation to presume capacity at 15 or older unless circumstances suggest otherwise; under 15, capacity is not presumed. A mature younger child is not categorically excluded. See [OAIC Chapter B, B.59–B.61](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-b-key-concepts).

## Current contact routes

These are the 27 September contact-form implementation choices, not age bands prescribed by legislation. [CONTACT_FORM.md](CONTACT_FORM.md) is the current field contract. The 15-year discussion above explains OAIC guidance and does not require a 15-year split in this request form.

| Person completing the form | Minimal route |
| --- | --- |
| Adult, 18 or older | Preferred name, mandatory contactable mobile, optional email and selected scheduling-contact method. Separate brief topic, suggested date/time, mode and contact/access needs are optional. |
| Self-requester under 18 | The same basic fields with age-appropriate contact agreement. A brief general non-sensitive topic and scheduling preferences are optional. LC establishes understanding, appropriate permission and willingness before interviewing. |
| Parent or guardian arranging participation for a child under 18 | The **adult’s** name, mobile and contact permissions; My child under 18 establishes the age band without asking again. The optional topic is what the child would like to discuss. No child’s name, date of birth or private history is needed for scheduling. |

A guardian's declaration is a record of what the adult states; it is not proof of guardianship or automatic consent to every later disclosure. The child's willingness still matters. A guardian arranging contact must not be presented as answering the child's own experience for them. If involving a parent may be unsafe, offer private staff contact; do not automatically notify a parent.

The request arranges contact; it is not blanket consent to an interview or to collect health information. [APP 3](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-3-app-3-collection-of-solicited-personal-information) distinguishes necessary ordinary personal information from sensitive information, for which consent is generally additionally required. Collect only what is reasonably necessary, by lawful and fair means. Any later sensitive discussion needs appropriate consent or another applicable legal basis. Brief-topic and contact/access prompts should not invite descriptions of abuse, diagnoses or other private circumstances, and no online capacity questionnaire is added.

## Notice and wording decisions

- **Questionnaire withdrawal:** replace the absolute statement that answers can never be changed or withdrawn with: **“You can stop before submitting. We do not collect a name or contact details for retrieving individual responses afterwards.”** Keep the general access, correction and complaints route. This explains the intended unlinked design without inviting identifying details. If unexpected identifiable content can reasonably be located, staff must consider applicable rights rather than mechanically refuse the request. See [APP 12](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-12-app-12-access-to-personal-information) and [APP 13](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-13-app-13-correction-of-personal-information).
- **Separation:** explain that the contact form is separate and does not receive questionnaire answers. Do not transfer answers, response IDs or other linkage in URLs or hidden fields. A conversation request does not depend on completing the questionnaire.
- **Contact purpose:** state that the form arranges a consultation conversation. It is not consent to recording, a service referral, an appointment confirmation or a promise that a particular service will be available. Consent to recording, if later proposed, belongs with the actual conversation.
- **Contact choice:** keep call/text/email scheduling choice and voicemail permission distinct. Only use the person's selected method and instructions; a required mobile is not permission for an extra call. Guardian requests should make clear whose name and number are requested.
- **Retention:** do not promise immediate deletion when someone cancels contact. Cancellation stops the requested follow-up; retention still follows applicable legal duties and the programme's justified records arrangements.
- **Free resource:** offering the same resource independently of answering or requesting a conversation avoids making access conditional on consent. The resource link should not carry form contents or participant identifiers.

The notice must identify the collector, purpose, significant consequences of not providing needed details, usual recipients, rights and complaint route, and applicable overseas disclosures. A layered notice is permitted; repeating a health/disability/safety warning beside every question is not a general legal requirement. A generic privacy-policy link does not replace programme-specific facts. See [APP 5, especially 5.5–5.6 and 5.18–5.33](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-5-app-5-notification-of-the-collection-of-personal-information).

## Safety and confidentiality

Keep a concise statement that information may be shared where law requires it or permits action to protect someone from serious harm. Do not promise absolute confidentiality. The notice does not need a lengthy catalogue of offences.

The [NT Care and Protection of Children Act 2007, section 26](https://legislation.nt.gov.au/api/sitecore/Act/PDF?id=11659) imposes reporting duties where a person forms the specified reasonable belief about child harm, exploitation or relevant sexual offences. Reports go to the CEO or police as soon as possible. The current [NT public guidance](https://nt.gov.au/law/crime/report-child-abuse) provides the reporting line, 1800 700 250. Mere selection of a support category does not establish the statutory facts.

For domestic violence, [section 124A of the NT Domestic and Family Violence Act 2007](https://legislation.nt.gov.au/api/sitecore/Act/PDF?id=11768) concerns an adult's reasonable belief about serious physical harm or a serious or imminent threat to life or safety. It includes reasonable-excuse provisions. It does not require an automatic police report for every mention of family conflict. Staff apply the actual threshold and circumstances; a form cannot replace that judgement.

## What Lutheran Care's public policy establishes

The current [Confidentiality and Privacy Policy and Procedure](https://www.lutherancare.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Confidentiality-and-Privacy-Policy-and-Procedure-2026_v12.pdf) is **version 12, June 2026**, approved 9 June 2026, with review due June 2028. Its live PDF was fetched and inspected after the web reader could not open it. Sections 6, 11, 13, 15 and 26 support necessary collection, capacity-sensitive consent, restricted access, approved organisational systems, rights and lawful retention/disposal. It does **not** identify this consultation’s receiving platform, hosting location or retention schedule. Section 13 distinguishes record categories; its financial-record example is not a survey retention period. The older 2021 PDF remains accessible but is superseded for this review. The NT reporting thresholds above come from current NT law, not a generalised policy summary.

The [NT Information Commissioner](https://infocomm.nt.gov.au/privacy/overview) describes the Information Act's privacy scheme as applying to NT public-sector organisations. It does not automatically govern every private charity solely because it operates in the NT. Relevant service contracts may impose additional obligations. The federal [Privacy Act 1988](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A03712/latest/text) and APPs are the appropriate core privacy framework for this design; this review does not infer the programme's contract terms.

## Formal presentation and real collection

The interface is being prepared in formal participant language for team review. There is presently no receiving endpoint for questionnaire answers or contact requests. The contact form displays the approved narrow review-only notice and ends with Finish preview; its completion does not claim receipt or a confirmed callback. This contact-specific presentation update does not alter the main questionnaire. Ordinary hosting request metadata is separate from entered form contents.

Before collecting real responses, confirm the actual Lutheran Care receiving system, who can access it, any third-party processing or overseas disclosures, the applicable retention/disposal arrangement, and the staff process for safe contact and children's participation. Then make the notice match those facts. This is a specific activation dependency, not a reason to withhold the review prototype. **“Stored securely by Lutheran Care” cannot itself establish where the data is held or who receives it.**

[APP 11](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-11-app-11-security-of-personal-information) requires reasonable technical and organisational protection and, subject to its exceptions, destruction or de-identification when information is no longer needed. A consent checkbox or disclaimer does not replace those arrangements. No additional HREC claim or research approval statement is introduced for this service-consultation brief.
