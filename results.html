03 / SUPPORT ADEQUACY

Was the help enough?

Some or no help = support gap

Support gap rate = (some help, but not enough + no help) ÷ (enough + some + none). Only definite adequacy answers for a selected need enter this rate. Unsure, prefer not to answer and skipped answers remain visible and are excluded.

Data, definitions & review notes+

What this dataset contains

Records are generated deterministically from a fixed seed. They contain no real people, no identifiers from participants and no free-text testimonials. Filters intersect; downloads contain only the records currently in view.

How to read these measures

  • Support need: selected an area in one multi-select question; its percentage uses all respondents in the view, including whole-question nonresponses. Selection is not a prevalence estimate.
  • Support gap: some help but not enough, or none. Enough help remains a reported need. The gap-rate denominator is shown for every row.
  • Item completion: recorded adequacy responses, including “unsure” and “prefer not to answer”, divided by all selected needs. Completion does not mean the answer is analytically definite.
  • Not selected: the area was not chosen. It does not imply no need. “None”, “unsure”, “prefer not to answer” and skipped describe the whole scan question.

Limits of the example

Adult, young-person and child questionnaires have different wording and domains, and are shown separately. Respondents are individuals, not unique households. Counts across domains overlap.

The default view includes only respondents living in NT. Family members elsewhere in Australia or overseas, and undisclosed locations, are separate filter options; extending scope is a team decision. For adults outside NT, the settling question asks about adjusting to a family member serving in NT; extended-scope views use a combined label. Relationship categories overlap. Financial and care dependence are separate adult-child answers in the export. No weighting, population estimates or statistical comparisons are applied.

Before collecting real data, agree eligibility, safeguarding, consent, storage, small-group disclosure rules and an analysis plan with the project team.
