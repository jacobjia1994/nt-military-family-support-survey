Questionnaire review guide

Start with the person’s background, select support areas once, and give as much optional detail as they wish. Then ask what could help next and how participation could work.

The adult questionnaire distinguishes support received from extra or different help wanted now for each selected area. The shorter shared questionnaire for ages 8–17 records broad needs first, then invites detail about one optional focus area.

The journey

  1. On the invitation page, choose Adult (18 or older) or Child or young person (under 18). The latter reveals 8–17 and 7 or younger. Nothing is preselected; the relevant participation information and choices appear inline. Ages 8–17 make an 8–14/15–17 choice for permission, then share the same questions.
  2. Confirm ADF relationship, optionally select the broad residential area, then optionally a suburb/locality within it. Adults then see optional local-life background; ages 8–17 have no separate place page. The youngest guardian route has its own short background step.
  3. First ask whether support was needed: Yes, No, Not sure or Prefer not to answer. Yes or Not sure reveals the accepted support-area checklist once.
  4. Adults complete detail for each selected area. Ages 8–17 may choose one optional focus area from their checked needs for detailed questions.
  5. Optional future programme interests and priorities, relevant child age groups, participation preferences, timing, enablers and Berrimah access for adults and young people, including those with no past support need.
  6. Review and finish, with a separate interview request and the free resource.

There is no second current-needs checklist, four-week impact score or repeated closing Your ideas question. Adding youth needs does not add repeated area pages. The earlier-experience route stays separate.

The support-area checklist is retained. All selected adult areas keep optional detailed questions because the survey substitutes for interviews the team cannot staff. No overseas-service transfer question is added.

QuestionInterpretation
How much of the support needed was received during the recall period?Enough, some but not enough, or none. Uncertain, declined and blank answers remain distinct.
Would the person like extra or different support now?A current request for additional or changed help. No does not mean existing ongoing help is unnecessary.
What support would help you now?Optional detail shown only after Yes, describing what the person wants.

Someone may have received insufficient housing support last year but need no extra help now. The same person may have received enough childcare support before, yet want a different arrangement after changing shifts. Both experiences can be recorded without selecting the needs again.

The needs question and visible optional questions

I did not need support in these areas is removed from the domain lists. The preceding needs-status answer records No separately. Only Yes or Not sure reveals a checklist. Selecting Something else opens its text input immediately underneath that checkbox. No skips detail questions but still leads adults and young people to future programme and participation questions.

In the adult route, the former More about this experience accordion is removed. Sources and experience are directly visible for every selected area, including enough past support or no extra help wanted now. The experience prompt is What happened when you needed support with this?, with a cue to describe what helped or what would have made things easier. In the 8–17 route, the checklist is broad and detailed questions concern only one optional focus area. Continue works with optional fields unanswered.

Sources and barriers refer to the same period as support received. People who sought help see barriers they experienced; explicit non-seeking shows reasons for not doing so. A blank, uncertain or declined source answer is not routed to either barrier list.

In the adult route, changing sources within the same help-seeking branch preserves barriers; changing between sought, not-sought and unknown/refused branches clears inapplicable barriers; changing a Yes current request clears its support-request text; removing an area removes its answer block. In the youth route, removing a checked focus need clears its detail without erasing other checked needs. Changing needs status to No, Prefer not to answer or blank clears dependent selections and answers without clearing general preferences. Unanswered fields mean missing information, not no difficulty.

Scope, reference periods and length

Current broad residential area and ADF relationship determine the route. Local areas use the full questionnaire even if the optional suburb is blank. Undisclosed area remains unspecified without exclusion. People now outside Greater Darwin can share past local experience separately; those explicitly reporting no past local residence, or no ADF family connection, see the scope explanation. Service-posting dates are not an eligibility proxy.

These routes define consultation participation, not programme-benefit eligibility. The consultation covers Greater Darwin, including Darwin, Palmerston, Litchfield, East Arm and Robertson Barracks. Two optional dropdowns record broad area first, then a suburb/locality from that area. The first offers Darwin, Palmerston, Litchfield / rural area, Another area in Greater Darwin, Outside Greater Darwin and Prefer not to say. The second lists relevant names alphabetically and offers Other with optional text. Leaving it blank preserves the area answer. Changing area clears old suburb details. Outside responses are separated from current local needs; choosing not to disclose does not automatically exclude someone. Area-only answers must not be allocated to a specific suburb: Palmerston alone is not Palmerston City. Duration concerns the current or most recent stay in Greater Darwin and does not add separate postings together.

Adults see 17 accepted support areas plus Something else. Ages 8–17 see one shared, age-accessible needs list. The checklist includes met needs and support still needed. The adult recall period is 12 months; the shared youth period is three months. These are project design choices, not validated recall windows.

After the invitation-page choices, the adult main route has 6 + n pages, where n is the number of selected areas. The shared 8–17 route has five pages without focus detail or six with it, including review. Earlier experience has three pages: connection, comments and review. These counts exclude the conditional guardian-presence screen and the separate form for ages 7 or younger. They are not completion-time estimates.

Future support and participation

Future programme interests are available even after No past need. Participation formats concern actual support or activities, not merely obtaining information. Timing appears for live formats; Berrimah access appears for in-person formats. Participation conditions remain optional and do not create a contact request or enrolment.

Adults and young people can answer even when they report no support needs. Preferences do not authorise contact, an interview, an appointment or a referral. Background questions precede the adult checklist; youth area and optional suburb/locality are combined with connection. The repeated closing ideas question has been removed; adult suggestions remain with each area and youth suggestions with the optional focus.

Children have their own perspective

The questionnaire retains its permission and assent process on the invitation page. Adults and young people aged 15–17 read the information and give their own agreement. Ages 8–14 need parent/guardian permission and their own separate choice. Children aged 7 or younger have a parent/guardian form with four optional child-expression boxes and a separate observation box. Child boxes require an adult’s confirmation that the child wants to join in; observations can be given without claiming child assent. The two perspectives stay separate.

On the connection page, ages 8–17 answer whether anyone is helping them read or write: No, I am answering myself, Yes, my parent or guardian or Yes, someone else. Ages 8–14 choosing self/other assistance must confirm that a parent or guardian is with them before substantive questions; the private LC help route remains available if this is unsafe or difficult. Ages 15–17 are not blocked for choosing another helper. Presence is this online form’s design policy, not a universal legal rule or a declaration that other helpers are invalid.

Eighteen is the age of majority. Fifteen is an operational capacity approach, not a universal legal consent age or a ban on younger participation. Staff consider understanding and support where needed. Parent permission cannot override refusal. There is no public worker-approved shortcut. Help or stop remains available; stopping clears current answers.

The separate contact form has parent/guardian and self-completion routes, including an under-15 request for an explanation. It does not replace consent to the actual consultation. A guardian supplies their own contact details; the limited younger-person route does not invite sensitive narratives or automatically trigger parent contact.

See the participation and safeguarding procedure, contact-form design, child-participation review and legal review. Helpers preserve the child’s own words; the questionnaire itself does not request helper identities or parental contact details.

Interpretation

Use each answered question’s relevant denominator. A blank or declined needs status, or a skipped checklist after Yes/Not sure, is not no need, and a visible but unanswered barrier question is not no barrier. Distinguish sought-help from non-seeking variants and past gaps from current requests. Current requests are not clinical assessments or rankings.

Records describe responses and participant perspectives, not verified unique individuals, unique households or NT population prevalence. Multiple family members may participate. Domain selections are multiple responses, so percentages can exceed 100%. Keep adult 12-month answers, shared youth three-month answers, guardian observations, uncertain NT connections and earlier-experience records distinguishable.

Consider frequency alongside gaps, current requests, valued support and what the programme or partners can change. The full question list shows wording and conditional fields; its review notes remain only in that page and can be downloaded. Participant walkthroughs remain the practical test of comprehension.

Current handling of answers

The participant pages use formal wording without draft or backend-status labels. Both forms hold entries in page memory, with no receiving endpoint or persistent answer store. Finishing does not transmit a response or claim receipt. The questionnaire completion screen offers the separate interview request and support finder, without an answer download or return-to-review action; contact details are not exported. Closing or refreshing clears entries.

Geography records broad area, optional suburb and location precision separately. The adult schema 7.1 record uses separate answers.needs_status, answers.needs and independent answers.areas[domain_id] blocks. The youth record keeps the broad needs selection distinct from one optional detailed focus. Earlier child/youth measures are not silently converted into the revised measure. Participation records carry context: internal_review and do not establish fieldwork approval.

Exports for ages 7 or younger use a separate young_child_supported record: child expressions and guardian observations remain distinct. An observation-only response makes no child-assent claim. These are not adult or youth support-area responses.

The v11 notice says people can stop before submitting and that names or contact details are not collected to retrieve individual responses afterwards. The general access, correction and complaint route remains available. Unexpected identifiable material still requires appropriate privacy and safeguarding handling; do not reconstruct identities for routine response retrieval.

Before real collection, LC must put the receiving system and agreed handling arrangements into operation, including access, any overseas processing, retention and safe contact. The legal review distinguishes these actual arrangements from notice wording. This is service consultation; no research/HREC approval statement is added. GitHub Pages may record ordinary visitor metadata even though entered answers are not transmitted.

Interview request and free resource

Request an interview with Lutheran Care appears in the questionnaire footer and thank-you screen. It opens the separate contact form in a new tab without answers, response IDs or a participant link. The introduction invites an interview with a Lutheran Care staff member to help LC understand Defence families’ difficulties and support needs. It is optional and does not require questionnaire completion.

The common resource is the standalone Defence family support finder. It appears at questionnaire completion and on the contact form and can also be opened directly. The completion page describes it as a free thank-you guide and uses the button Find support in a few clicks, alongside the interview request with equal styling. The navigator itself remains NT-wide. It does not require contact consent, email capture or completing optional questions, and carries no participant identifiers.

The example-results page contains fictional records from an older instrument. It does not analyse schema 7.1 answers or report real findings.

Source material

The areas and examples were checked against the supplied Defence and Veteran Family Wellbeing Strategy 2025–2030 and First Action Plan. See the page-specific mapping. The examples aid recognition; they do not exhaust every experience or establish NT prevalence and programme entitlements.

The notice draws on LC’s feedback form, referral forms and June 2026 privacy policy, v12. A comparable public community questionnaire was not located; the complaints receiver is not assumed to receive this consultation. See source notes.

The design also draws on AIFS needs assessment guidance, AIFS guidance on involving children and ABS Forms Design Standards. The wording is project-specific, not a validated scale.
