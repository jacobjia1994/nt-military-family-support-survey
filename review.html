Questionnaire review guide

Start with the person’s background, select support areas once, then complete each area.

The questionnaire distinguishes support received from extra or different help wanted now. Each area has its own answers, including what worked and what could improve. Its optional questions appear directly on the page.

The journey

  1. Invitation, age-appropriate participation and the minimum NT connection check.
  2. Optional About you questions: broad residence, current/most-recent NT stay, military affiliation and younger respondents’ assistance with answering.
  3. Choose support areas once from the accepted list.
  4. Complete each selected area: past support, current request, what support would help if Yes, sources, relevant barriers/reasons, and experience.
  5. Preferences for finding information or advice about services and support in the NT, for adults and young people.
  6. Review and finish, with a separate conversation request and the free resource.

There is no second current-needs checklist, four-week impact score or repeated closing Your ideas question. Children continue from their area questions to review. The earlier-NT-experience route stays separate.

Past support and support wanted now

QuestionInterpretation
How much of the support needed was received during the recall period?Enough, some but not enough, or none. Uncertain, declined and blank answers remain distinct.
Would the person like extra or different support now?A current request for additional or changed help. No does not mean existing ongoing help is unnecessary.
What support would help you now?Optional detail shown only after Yes, describing what the person wants.
Sources, barriers/reasons, and what worked or could improveExperience within the selected area, including earlier difficulties that have since been resolved.

Someone may have received insufficient housing support last year but need no extra help now. The same person may have received enough childcare support before, yet want a different arrangement after changing shifts. Both experiences can be recorded without selecting the needs again.

Visible optional questions

The former More about this experience accordion is removed. Sources and experience are directly visible for every selected area, including enough past support or no extra help wanted now. Continue works with optional fields unanswered.

Sources and barriers refer to the same period as support received. People who sought help see barriers they experienced; explicit non-seeking shows reasons for not doing so. A blank, uncertain or declined source answer is not routed to either barrier list.

Changing sources clears only that area’s dependent barriers. Changing a Yes current request to another answer clears its support-request text. Removing an area removes its answer block. Unanswered fields mean missing information, not no difficulty. Reported concerns about trust, privacy or eligibility do not prove a service failure or refusal.

Scope, reference periods and length

Current NT service or service ending within the past 12 months leads to the full questionnaire. Earlier service leads to one optional lessons/suggestions page, then review and finish. Uncertain connections remain uncertain; no NT service connection leads to a scope explanation.

These routes define consultation participation, not programme-benefit eligibility. Family members can live elsewhere. Broad residence is optional and does not exclude people. NT duration concerns the current or most recent stay, includes a never-lived-in-NT option, and does not add separate postings together.

Adults see 17 accepted support areas plus Something else. Youth and child lists remain age-appropriate. The checklist includes met needs and support still needed. The adult/youth recall period is 12 months; the child period is 3 months. Support received, sources and barriers use that window; extra or different support is asked about now. These are project design choices, not validated recall windows.

After invitation, age and participation, the adult/youth main route has 5 + n pages, where n is the number of selected areas: 5 with none, 7 with two, 10 with five, including review. Children have 4 + n. Earlier experience has three pages: connection, comments and review. These are page counts, not completion-time estimates.

Finding services and support in the NT

The preferences question explicitly concerns information or advice about services and support in the NT. Phone, video, in-person and written formats describe information and navigation, not how housing or childcare will be delivered. Timing appears only after a synchronous format is selected.

Adults and young people can answer even when they select no needs. Preferences do not authorise contact, an interview, an appointment or a referral. Background questions now come before the needs checklist. The repeated closing ideas question has been removed; suggestions remain with each area.

Children have their own perspective

The questionnaire retains its permission and assent process. Adults and young people aged 15–17 read the information and give their own agreement. Ages 7–14 first see parent/guardian permission, followed by the child’s separate choice. Under 7s have a facilitated conversation guide with permission and the child’s willing participation.

Eighteen is the age of majority. Fifteen is an operational capacity approach, not a universal legal consent age or a ban on younger participation. Staff consider understanding and support where needed. Parent permission cannot override refusal. There is no public worker-approved shortcut. Help or stop remains available; stopping clears current answers.

The separate contact form has parent/guardian and self-completion routes, including an under-15 request for an explanation. It does not replace consent to the actual consultation. A guardian supplies their own contact details; the limited younger-person route does not invite sensitive narratives or automatically trigger parent contact.

See the participation and safeguarding procedure, contact-form design and legal review. Helpers preserve the child’s own words; the questionnaire itself does not request helper identities or parental contact details.

Interpretation

Use each answered question’s relevant denominator. A skipped needs checklist is not no need, and a visible but unanswered barrier question is not no barrier. Distinguish sought-help from non-seeking variants and past gaps from current requests. Current requests are not clinical assessments or rankings.

Records describe individuals, not unique households or NT population prevalence. Multiple family members may participate. Domain selections are multiple responses, so percentages can exceed 100%. Keep age versions, uncertain NT connections and earlier-experience records distinguishable; do not pool different measures as though they were identical.

Consider frequency alongside gaps, current requests, valued support and what the programme or partners can change. The full question list shows wording and conditional fields; its review notes remain only in that page and can be downloaded. Participant walkthroughs remain the practical test of comprehension.

Current handling of answers

The participant pages use formal wording without draft or backend-status labels. Both forms hold entries in page memory, with no receiving endpoint or persistent answer store. Finishing does not transmit a response or claim receipt. Questionnaire respondents can download a local JSON copy; contact details are not exported. Closing or refreshing clears entries.

Schema 5 uses answers.needs and independent answers.areas[domain_id] blocks: received support, current request, Yes-only support_requested, sources, barriers and comment. Older collective/current-only records are not automatically migrated; the removed global anything answer is not exported. Participation records carry context: internal_review and do not establish fieldwork approval.

The v10 notice says people can stop before submitting and that names or contact details are not collected to retrieve individual responses afterwards. The general access, correction and complaint route remains available. Unexpected identifiable material still requires appropriate privacy and safeguarding handling; do not reconstruct identities for routine response retrieval.

Before real collection, LC must put the receiving system and agreed handling arrangements into operation, including access, any overseas processing, retention and safe contact. The legal review distinguishes these actual arrangements from notice wording. This is service consultation; no research/HREC approval statement is added. GitHub Pages may record ordinary visitor metadata even though entered answers are not transmitted.

Conversation request and free resource

Arrange a conversation appears in the questionnaire footer and thank-you screen. It opens the separate contact form in a new tab without answers, response IDs or a participant link. It is an optional consultation request and does not require questionnaire completion.

Configure the common resource in thank-you-resource.js when the team has made it. It appears at questionnaire completion and on the contact form, with the same public HTTPS link for everyone. Until a URL is supplied, the inactive button says Available soon. It does not require contact consent, email capture or completing optional questions, and carries no participant identifiers.

The example-results page contains fictional records from an older instrument. It does not analyse schema 5 answers or report real findings.

Source material

The areas and examples were checked against the supplied Defence and Veteran Family Wellbeing Strategy 2025–2030 and First Action Plan. See the page-specific mapping. The examples aid recognition; they do not exhaust every experience or establish NT prevalence and programme entitlements.

The notice draws on LC’s feedback form, referral forms and June 2026 privacy policy, v12. A comparable public community questionnaire was not located; the complaints receiver is not assumed to receive this consultation. See source notes.

The design also draws on AIFS needs assessment guidance, AIFS guidance on involving children and ABS Forms Design Standards. The wording is project-specific, not a validated scale.
