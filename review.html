Questionnaire review guide

Select support areas once, complete each area, then talk about future arrangements.

The questionnaire asks about support received and extra or different help wanted now. Each area has its own answers. A person can describe a past difficulty that has been resolved, a current request, or both.

The journey

  1. Invitation, age-appropriate participation and the minimum NT connection check.
  2. Choose support areas once from the accepted list.
  3. Complete each selected area: support received in the recall period, then extra or different support wanted now. Optional details stay on that page.
  4. General information and advice preferences for adults and young people, including people who selected no needs.
  5. One optional question about useful support or other suggestions.
  6. Optional background questions, then review and finish.

There is no second current-needs checklist or four-week impact score. Children retain an age-appropriate ending without the adult contact-format preferences. The completion resource remains available after finishing.

Two different questions in each area

What we askWhat it tells us
How much of the support needed was received during the recall period?Whether the person received enough support, some but not enough, or none. Uncertain, declined and blank answers remain distinct.
Would the person like extra or different support now?A current request for additional or changed help. “No” does not mean existing ongoing help is unnecessary.
Optional sources, barriers/reasons and a comment about that areaContext from people who choose to elaborate, including those whose earlier difficulty was resolved.

For example, someone may have received insufficient housing support last year but need no extra help now. The same person may have received enough childcare support before, yet want a different arrangement after changing shifts. The questionnaire records both without asking them to select their needs again.

Optional detail

More about this experience is available on every selected area's page. Opening it is optional and does not depend on an unmet need or a current request. Continue works without opening or answering these fields.

Sources and barriers refer to the same past period as support received. People who sought help see barriers they experienced. People who explicitly did not seek help see reasons for not doing so. A blank, uncertain or declined source answer does not imply an attempt and is not routed to either barrier list.

Changing the source response clears only that area's dependent barriers. Deselecting an area removes its answer block. An optional blank is missing information, not a report of no difficulty. Concerns about trust, privacy or eligibility are reported concerns; they do not prove a service failure or an actual refusal of eligibility.

Scope and reference periods

NT connection and residence

Current NT service or service ending within the past 12 months leads to the full questionnaire. An earlier connection leads to one optional lessons/suggestions page, followed by review and finish. Those records stay separate from recent-needs measures. Uncertain connections remain uncertain; no NT service connection leads to a scope explanation.

These are consultation routes, not programme-benefit eligibility rules. Family members can live elsewhere. Broad residence is optional. NT residence duration concerns the current or most recent stay, includes a never-lived-in-NT option, and does not add separate postings together.

Needs, recall and length

Adults see 17 accepted support areas plus “Something else”. Youth and child options remain shorter and age-appropriate. The checklist includes needs that were met and needs that continue now. Exclusive none, uncertain and declined choices remain available.

The adult and youth recall period is 12 months; the child period is 3 months. Support received, sources and barriers use that period. Extra or different support is asked about now. These are project design choices, not validated recall windows.

After invitation, age and participation, the adult main route has 6 pages with no areas, 8 with two, and 11 with five, including review. Each selected area adds one page, with optional detail on that page. These are page counts, not completion-time estimates.

General preferences and suggestions

Phone, video, in-person and written formats refer to getting information and advice. They do not describe how housing, childcare or other substantive services would be delivered. Timing appears only after a synchronous format is selected.

Adults and young people can answer these questions even if they report no needs. Preferences are not consent to contact, an interview opt-in, an appointment or a referral request. The closing question invites support worth keeping as well as other suggestions. Optional military affiliation, broad residence, NT stay and assistance with answering come afterwards.

Children have their own perspective

Adults and young people aged 15–17 read the information and give their own agreement. Ages 7–14 first see parent/guardian permission, followed by a separate choice for the child. Under 7s have a facilitated conversation guide with permission and the child's willing participation.

This age split is an operational design choice, not a universal legal consent rule. Anyone unsure about understanding, authority or safe guardian involvement can speak with an LC worker first. Parent permission cannot override a child's refusal. There is no public “worker approved” shortcut. Help or stop is available throughout; stopping clears current answers.

Use the staff participation and safeguarding procedure for individual capacity assessment, assistance and handling disclosures. Helpers preserve the child's own answers. The questionnaire does not request a helper's identity or a parent's contact details.

Read answers with the right denominator

  • Area selected: report selection against respondents in the stated cohort. A skipped checklist is not an explicit “no need”.
  • Past support gap: some or no support among definite support-received answers for that area. Report uncertain, declined and missing separately.
  • Extra or different support wanted: describe the current request for each area, separate from past support. It is not a clinical assessment or a ranking.
  • Sources and barriers: use the number actually answering the relevant optional question. People who left details closed cannot be counted as having no barrier. Distinguish sought-help and non-seeking variants.
  • People, not families: multiple members of a family may participate. Domain selections are multiple responses, so percentages can total more than 100%.
  • Different routes: retain age versions, uncertain NT connections and earlier-experience records separately. Do not pool different questions as though they measured the same thing.

Frequency alone should not determine action. Consider support gaps, current requests, less visible groups, valued support and what the programme or partners can change. These self-selected responses describe participants, not population prevalence across the NT.

Useful review walkthroughs

  1. Select Housing and Childcare. Report insufficient past housing help with no extra help wanted now, and sufficient past childcare help with extra help wanted now. Check that both experiences remain clear.
  2. Open optional details for a resolved need. Check that a helpful service or past barrier can still be described.
  3. Select no needs. Continue to general preferences, suggestions and optional background without difficulty modules.
  4. Select five areas. Check that each is completed once and optional detail can be left alone.
  5. Try one area with help sought, one with explicit non-seeking, and one with the source answer left blank. Check the different barrier routes.
  6. Try a family member outside the NT, an earlier NT connection, and the child participation process.
  7. Return to an earlier page and remove one area or change one source answer. Check that unrelated answers remain intact.

The full question list shows current wording, options and conditional fields. Its review notes are kept only in that page; download them before closing. Read questions aloud and note unclear wording, missing options or information whose use is not clear.

Current handling of answers

This is the intended formal interface for internal review. The participant pages do not display draft or backend-status labels. Answers stay in page memory: there is no submission endpoint, account, application analytics, local storage or contact form. Finishing does not transmit a response. A respondent can download a local JSON copy; refreshing or closing the page clears entered answers.

Schema 4 records one area selection in answers.needs and independent responses in answers.areas[domain_id]: support received, extra/different support wanted now, sources, barriers and comment. Earlier collective or current-only records must not be imported as though they answered these questions. Exported participation records carry context: internal_review; they do not establish fieldwork approval.

LC must put its institution-managed receiver and agreed handling arrangements into operation before real collection. The project is service consultation, as confirmed by Jacob; apply LC's consultation, privacy and safeguarding procedures. Research/HREC review is not a current prerequisite. Any later research use would need separate consideration.

The intended design keeps no routine link from answers to names or contact details, so individual withdrawal after submission is unavailable. Do not reconstruct identity from narratives for routine retrieval. Unexpected identifying disclosures still need appropriate privacy and safeguarding handling. GitHub Pages records visitors' IP addresses for security even though answers are not transmitted.

Completion resource

Configure the resource in thank-you-resource.js when the team has made it. It appears after completion, including optional blank answers and the earlier-experience route, and uses the same public HTTPS link for everyone. Until a URL is supplied, the inactive button says Available soon. No email capture, response-linked download or gift content is invented.

The example-results page contains fictional records from the earlier single-priority instrument. It does not analyse schema 4 answers or report real findings.

Source material

The adult areas and examples were checked against the supplied Defence and Veteran Family Wellbeing Strategy 2025–2030 and First Action Plan. See the page-specific mapping. The 17 areas and concise examples aid recognition; they do not exhaust every experience or separately measure every example. The national source does not establish NT prevalence or programme entitlements.

The participant notice adapts the LC feedback form, referral forms and June 2026 privacy policy. A comparable public community survey was not located. The complaints system is not assumed to receive this consultation's answers. See source and adaptation notes.

The approach draws on AIFS: Needs assessment, AIFS: Involving children in evaluation and ABS Forms Design Standards. The wording is project-specific, not a validated scale. Participant walkthroughs remain the practical test of comprehension.
