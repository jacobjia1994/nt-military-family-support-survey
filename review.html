Questionnaire review guide

What the questions ask, how the different paths work and what the team needs to review.

The questionnaire follows one idea: understand everyday life, recognise support that works, identify unmet needs, then explore each current need separately. The results page uses fictional records to show how analysis would work.

Start with the wording

The full question list shows the actual wording, every option and the conditional questions. Choose an age version and location. You can add review notes beside each section and download them as a text file; notes are not sent anywhere or saved after this page is closed.

Read a few questions aloud. Mark anything you would not naturally say to a participant, any option that is missing, and anything that feels repetitive or too personal for its purpose. Use the section names when discussing changes.

Three decisions for the team

  1. Who will we invite? The demo includes relatives living outside the NT, with their answers treated separately. Confirm whether they belong in this consultation.
  2. Which age groups will take part first? All paths are available for staff review. Decide whether to start with adults or also use the proposed younger-person pathways once participation arrangements are agreed.
  3. Who will introduce the survey and receive the answers? Agree the organisation, contact and data arrangements before replacing this demo with a live questionnaire.

How the paths work

NT connection and participation

Current and former members and their families can contribute. Current NT service or service ending in the past 12 months leads to the full questionnaire. An earlier connection leads to one optional comments page, recorded separately. Uncertain connections remain uncertain in analysis. No NT service leads to a scope explanation.

This defines consultation routes, not eligibility for programme benefits. Residence is optional and never excludes family members elsewhere. NT residence duration refers to the current or most recent stay only, with a never-lived-in-NT option. Do not add several postings together.

Needs, adequacy and the no-extra-support path

Adults can select 16 support areas, plus “Something else”. Young people and children see shorter, age-appropriate lists. “No need”, “Not sure” and “Prefer not to answer” are exclusive choices.

Only selected areas receive an adequacy question. The choices are enough support, some but not enough, none, not sure, and prefer not to answer. A blank answer stays blank.

Everyone can say that no additional support is needed now. That choice skips impact, help-seeking, barriers and delivery questions. A need met in the past is not treated as a current problem.

Help sought versus reasons for not seeking help

People who have sought help are asked about barriers they actually experienced. People who have not sought help see a separate set of reasons for that decision. Unknown help-seeking status is retained separately.

For young people, a reported concern about trust or privacy is a concern they experienced; it does not prove that a service disclosed information or failed them. Each current need now has a separate answer block. A change in help seeking clears barriers only for that same need. Do not transfer an answer from another need or from the previous collective version. Eligibility concerns and being told one is ineligible should remain distinct.

Reference periods and length

Past needs and adequacy use 12 months for adults and young people, 3 months for children. Per-need impact uses 4 weeks. The annual window aims to include postings, school-year changes and recently departed families; it is a project design choice and can involve more recall error than a shorter period.

Each selected current need gets one page. Impact, help sought and desired change are optional; the barrier/reason question appears only after a substantive help-seeking response. Delivery/time preferences are asked once, in general. Pilot timing should determine the eventual completion estimate.

Children have their own perspective

The form now separates the participation process from age-appropriate questions. Adults and young people aged 15–17 read the information and give their own agreement. Ages 7–14 first see parent/guardian permission, followed by a separate choice for the child. Under 7s have a facilitated conversation guide with permission and the child's willing participation.

The age split is an operational design choice, not a universal legal consent rule. Anyone unsure about understanding, authority or safe guardian involvement can speak with an LC worker first. There is no public “worker approved” shortcut. Parent permission cannot override a child's refusal. Help or stop is available throughout the questionnaire and stopping clears current answers.

Use the staff participation and safeguarding procedure for individual capacity assessment, assistance, review of actual responses and disclosures. Assistance preserves the child's own answers; no helper identity or parent's contact details are requested in the questionnaire.

Read results with the right denominator

  • Need selected: people selecting an area, divided by respondents in that view. Not selected is not the same as an explicit “no need”.
  • Support gap: some or no support, among definite adequacy answers for that area. Show unsure, declined and skipped answers separately.
  • Current needs: multiple responses per person, so percentages may total more than100%. These are not rankings. “No additional support now” remains a meaningful exclusive answer.
  • Respondent counts: individuals, not unique families. Multiple members of one family may participate.
  • Separate versions: youth and child wording and domains differ. Do not pool their answers into adult percentages.

Frequency alone should not determine action. Consider impact, unmet need, less visible groups, what families value, and what the project or its partners can change. Self-selected responses describe those who took part; they do not establish population prevalence across the NT.

Useful internal review routes

  1. An adult partner from another country’s military, living in Katherine, selecting several past needs and several current needs.
  2. An independently living adult child, and then an adult child who relies on financial or care support.
  3. A person with no past support needs, or whose past needs were fully met.
  4. A person who needed help but did not seek it.
  5. A young person or child sharing their own experience with reading assistance.
  6. A family member outside the NT. Check that the wording follows their location.
  7. Return to an earlier page and change needs, priority or help-seeking. Check that irrelevant follow-up answers are removed.

Current handling of answers

Answers are held in page memory only. There is no submission endpoint, account, analytics tracker, local storage or contact form. Finishing does not transmit a response. A respondent can download their own JSON file. Refreshing or closing the page clears entered answers.

The results page reads only fictional records packaged with this site. Before real collection, connect an institution-managed collection service and replace the information about answer handling with the actual agreed arrangements.

Law, ethics and service preferences

The participant view shows the intended formal survey for internal review, as requested. It does not label itself as a draft or display backend-status text. This staff guide records that no responses are transmitted. Demonstrated consent/permission records are marked internal_review in exports. The staff procedure gives the concrete participation, assistance and disclosure arrangements to use with LC’s approved receiver. Jacob has confirmed that this project is service consultation. Apply LC’s consultation, privacy and safeguarding procedures; research/HREC review is not a current prerequisite. Any later research use would require separate consideration.

Service preferences describe how someone might get help from Lutheran Care or another relevant service. They are not an interview opt-in, appointment, referral request or promise to attend. Information/navigation and contact formats are separate kinds of preference within one broad list. Interpret the impact question descriptively, not as a clinical distress scale. Help-seeking timing is not specified; do not report it as annual prevalence.

The generic caring and adult-child dependency questions have been removed. Relevant childcare, caring and disability support options remain. GitHub Pages records visitors’ IP addresses for security even though questionnaire answers are not transmitted.

Completion resource and answer handling

Configure the future resource in thank-you-resource.js. It appears after completion, including when optional fields are blank, and opens the same public HTTPS link for everyone. Until a URL is supplied, the inactive button says Available soon. No gift content, email capture or response-linked download is invented. The intended data design keeps no link from ordinary answers to names or contact details; individual post-submission withdrawal is therefore unavailable. Do not reconstruct identity from narratives for routine lookup.

The fictional results use the earlier single-priority instrument. Do not treat them as analyses of current schema 3 per-need records.

Strategy basis

The adult needs and examples were checked against the supplied Defence and Veteran Family Wellbeing Strategy 2025–2030. See the page-specific mapping. Information/service navigation was missing; postings/leaving service, grief, education, housing, care and social connection are now stated in concrete examples. The national source does not establish NT prevalence, programme entitlements or validated recall windows.

Existing Lutheran Care practice

The participant notice adapts the actual LC feedback form, current referral forms and June 2026 privacy policy. A comparable public community survey was not located. The complaint system is not assumed to be the receiver for this consultation. See source and adaptation notes.

Method references

The design draws on AIFS: Needs assessment, AIFS: Involving children in evaluation, and ABS: Questionnaire design. Wording here is project-specific and requires user testing; it is not a validated psychometric scale.
