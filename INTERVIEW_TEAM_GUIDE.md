# Interview team guide

27 September 2026 · Operating template for the Greater Darwin Defence Family Support consultation

Use one coordinator, one backup and one stream of private interviews, grouped into available appointment windows. Offer phone and video where the team can provide them. This is a practical proposal for discussion with Lutheran Care (LC); it does not require an existing receiver to be reviewed or demonstrated. The current website is a preview: entered details are not sent or saved, and no callback is being arranged. The templates below are for future use if LC adopts the arrangement and supplies the actual operating details; they are not messages already sent.

The four team members need not attend every interview. Being a student or a Defence family member does not establish or rule out professional capability. Allocate interviewing, note-taking, coordination and safeguarding duties according to training, authorised role, existing relationships and the participant’s preference. Describe the actual interviewer’s role, including student status where relevant, before confirming a meeting.

Prepare only appointment windows that people can actually staff. An adult conversation will usually be offered as **30–40 minutes**, with the duration confirmed when arranging it; allow about 15 minutes between appointments for notes, a break and a private arrival/departure. Start with a manageable block, review the workload, then open further slots. A preference in the form is not a booking or a promise that every mode is available.

Use a quiet, accessible LC or suitable community room with private conversations. Send each person their own time; do not use a shared attendance list or invite everyone to queue. For phone/video, the interviewer also needs a private space. Ask about practical arrangements without promising childcare, transport, interpreters or other support that has not been organised. A support person may attend by agreement; a couple’s joint conversation is optional and does not replace either person’s individual perspective.

## Handle a request once, clearly

1. **Check and allocate.** The coordinator checks requests at the stated times, reads safe-contact instructions and assigns an owner and next action. Every route requires a genuine, contactable **mobile number**; email is optional and never replaces it. A format-valid number is not verified ownership or reachability. Establish contact through the authorised first-contact method; do not call a mandatory mobile merely because it was supplied when email or text was selected.
2. **Arrange the time in one useful exchange.** Through the selected text, email or call channel, offer two or three actual options together with duration, mode/location, interviewer roles and an easy cancellation route. If no option works, offer the next available window or ask whether the person wants to wait. This is scheduling, not a preliminary interview: there is no compulsory extra phone call before the interview. A phone-interview preference does not override a request to arrange it by text or email.
3. **Confirm in the same private exchange.** Agree on one appointment and any practical arrangements. Confirm that the mobile is usable for agreed appointment contact; do not require identity documents or an automated verification service. Permission to use a new contact channel must be obtained before using it. Tell the person if there will be a note-taker or anyone they may know and offer another arrangement without requiring a reason. Confirmation can be the coordinator’s reply to the chosen slot; it does not require a separate call.
4. **Use finite follow-up.** Send at most one unanswered-invitation reminder, through the permitted channel, at the agreed interval. Give a clear reply date. If there is still no reply, close as “no response”; do not record this as “no need” or continue chasing. Stop immediately if contact is cancelled. For a confirmed appointment, offer one reminder using the agreed channel; for a missed appointment, offer one chance to rebook and then close if unanswered.
5. **Manage capacity explicitly.** The form prominently explains that limited project time and staffing may prevent contact or an interview for every registrant, and records a separate required acknowledgement. Do not interpret this as a waiver or permission to contact through a different channel. Review requests within the team’s capacity; registration does not promise an individual response. If available slots are full, say so. Ask before adding someone to a waitlist, record its end date and do not call it a confirmed appointment. At the end, tell those still waiting that this round has closed, using their permitted channel unless they have asked for no further contact. A later consultation needs an appropriate fresh invitation, not an indefinite waiting list.
6. **Interview, summarise and close.** Confirm willingness and the information below, hold the conversation, read back key points, thank the participant and record completion. Keep any safeguarding action in LC’s restricted process, not the ordinary appointment log. Cancellation stops follow-up but does not promise destruction of records that LC must retain.

Do not batch participants into the same email, text thread or meeting invitation. First-contact choice, voicemail permission, safe hours and later channel agreements apply to reminders and closure messages as well as the first contact. Never treat an optional email address as consent to marketing or unrelated future projects.

## People, relationships and participation

**An adult describing their parenting or caring experience is an adult participant.** Use the child/guardian route only when arranging for the child to participate personally. On that route, the guardian supplies their own mobile and contact details; My child under 18 establishes the broad age group without another age question. Do not collect the child’s name, date of birth, diagnosis or private history in the request.

The interview request asks self-requesters only Under 18 / 18 or older. Someone under 18 may optionally supply a brief general topic and suggest a date/time and interview mode, just as on the other routes; save sensitive detail for the interview. The age choice and contact agreement do not establish capacity or full interview consent. Before a young person is interviewed, the designated LC worker establishes understanding, appropriate permission and the child’s willingness under [the consultation procedure](consultation-procedure.md), asking for further age information only if needed. A guardian declaration alone is not verified authority or the child’s agreement. Do not automatically notify a parent about a self-request. Do not use the adult duration or guide unchanged for a child; arrange a suitable conversation with the responsible worker. Professional contributors use the same self/age route. The main survey’s separate age/permission arrangements are unaffected.

Defence family team members can improve wording, accessibility, outreach and interpretation, and can interview where competent and authorised. Give them access to identifiable information only where their assigned task needs it. They may know participants; offer another interviewer when a relationship could inhibit speaking freely. Their own lived experience is valuable but does not represent all families. If a team member also contributes an interview, label that role clearly and avoid counting the same account more than once.

Recruit beyond the team’s own friends where possible. Seek different circumstances and both helpful and difficult experiences, including people who have not used services. Do not add extensive demographic questions just to create an elaborate quota. After the first block, ask which perspective or practical uncertainty remains missing and target a small number of further conversations accordingly.

## Adult and family conversation: 30–40 minutes

**Opening.** Introduce everyone and their role. Explain that this is voluntary consultation to help LC plan its Greater Darwin programme, not counselling, an assessment for benefits or a service appointment. Participation does not affect access to support. The person can skip questions, pause or stop. Explain who will read notes, how findings will be reported without names, and relevant confidentiality limits. Confirm consent to the conversation and to note-taking. Requesting contact was not interview or recording consent.

Use brief structured notes as the default. Do not record or transcribe automatically. If a specific purpose requires recording or attributable quotations, explain it and obtain separate appropriate agreement first. Do not promise absolute anonymity: avoid identifying details in reports, particularly in a small community.

Use these prompts flexibly; follow the person’s story rather than asking every prompt as a checklist.

1. **Life here:** What has life in Greater Darwin been like for you or your family? What has helped you feel settled, connected or supported?
2. **One real experience:** Can you tell us about a time when support worked well, or when you wanted support and it was difficult to get? What was happening, and what did you do first? A general example is fine; no names or sensitive detail are needed.
3. **What made the difference:** What helped? Where did things become difficult? Did knowing where to go, eligibility, timing, cost, transport, childcare, privacy or the way you were treated play a part? Use only prompts relevant to what was said.
4. **People not reached:** Was there support you decided not to use, or could not access? What would have made it easier? Avoid assuming everyone wants a service or currently has unmet needs.
5. **A useful change:** If LC could improve one practical thing for families here, what would matter most? What should it keep, work with or avoid duplicating? What would make that idea usable for you?
6. **Close the loop:** What have we missed? Summarise two or three points and ask whether that captures their meaning. Explain the confirmed plan for sharing findings; do not promise that every suggestion will be implemented.

If someone becomes distressed or discloses harm, pause and use the existing LC procedure. Do not seek details just to complete these prompts. A referral or service response, where wanted and appropriate, is a separate action with the relevant permissions.

## Professional conversations: a separate perspective

Professionals who use the form select Me and their age group, then provide the same contact details as other self-requesters. They may mention their work in the dedicated optional discussion-topic field; no separate professional branch, ADF-connection checklist or organisation/role question is needed to book a time. Record the relevant role at the interview. Keep topic, suggested date/time and mode separate in the form and records, because they support different decisions. One contact/access-needs field covers safe contact and practical participation requirements. Use the same coordinator and private appointment log. Direct professional invitations should target people who understand a relevant service interface: for example, family-support coordination, community services, primary or mental health care, schools or childcare. Choose by the actual question being investigated, not seniority. Start with a few different interfaces and add others only where a gap remains.

Ask whether the person is describing professional experience, their own family experience, or both. Keep those perspectives distinguishable. A professional opinion cannot cancel a family’s account that an existing service was difficult to find or use; the difference is useful evidence about access. Named quotations or claims to speak officially for an organisation require appropriate permission. Do not ask for identifiable client stories or records.

**Invitation template — fill before sending through an authorised channel**

> Hello [name], I am [name and role] with Lutheran Care’s Greater Darwin Defence Family Support consultation team. We would value your practical perspective on [specific service interface] to help us understand what is working, where families encounter difficulties and how LC could contribute. Would you be available for a conversation of [confirmed duration] by [available mode]? We can offer [actual options]. Participation is voluntary. We will discuss service patterns, not identifiable client cases, and explain how notes and findings will be used before starting. Please reply by [date] to [project contact], or let us know if you would prefer not to be contacted again.

**Short guide**

1. Which Defence members or families do you actually reach, and who tends to be missed?
2. Where do people enter your service, and where do referrals or handovers most often stall?
3. What already works well? What eligibility, capacity, waiting-time or practical constraints should LC understand?
4. What could LC usefully add or coordinate without duplicating services? What would make that feasible?
5. When LC refers someone, what would a workable handover look like? What should we check rather than assume?
6. What important perspective is missing from our consultation? Summarise the key points for correction.

A later optional group can help test service ideas or practical arrangements. It is not the default venue for personal accounts. Keep power relationships in mind, and explain that other participants hear what is said; do not mix families and organisational decision-makers simply to save interview time.

## Appointment messages

Use the authorised channel and safe wording. Replace every bracket before sending. Do not send a voicemail unless the separate permission allows it. These are draft templates, not automatic notifications.

**Initial family invitation**

> Hello [preferred name], this is [name and role] from the LC consultation team. Thank you for your interest in sharing your experience of Defence family life in Greater Darwin. Would [option one] or [option two] suit you for a private conversation of about 30–40 minutes by [available mode/venue]? [Interviewer name and role] will talk with you [and optional note-taker/role]. You can ask for another interviewer or arrangement, decline, or change your mind. Please reply by [date] using [safe project contact]. If we do not hear back, we will send one reminder and then stop contacting you about this invitation.

**Confirmation**

> Your consultation conversation is arranged for [date, time and time zone], for about [agreed duration], by [phone/video/private venue details]. You will speak with [name, role and anyone else attending]. [Confirmed practical arrangements.] You do not need to prepare anything. You can skip questions, pause or stop. To cancel or change the time, use [agreed project contact]. [Agreed reminder arrangement.] Please let us know if your contact instructions change.

**One invitation reminder / one rebooking offer**

> Hello [preferred name], this is [name] from the LC consultation team. [We are checking whether you would still like to take part / Would you like another time for the conversation?] Please reply through [safe contact] by [date] if so. If we do not hear back, we will close this invitation and will not keep following up. It is fine to decline.

**Cancellation**

> Thanks for letting us know. Your appointment for [date/time] is cancelled. [We have also stopped further contact as requested / If you would like another time, you can contact us at the agreed project contact.] No explanation is needed.

**Full / waiting / closing this round**

> [The available appointments are currently full. Would you like us to keep your request on a waiting list until [date], using your existing contact preferences? This would not be a confirmed appointment.] [This consultation round has now closed and we cannot offer another appointment. Thank you for your interest; we will not keep contacting you about this request.] [Confirmed public findings location, if available.]

## Minimal records and handover

One row per request is enough. This is a schema only; never enter real rows in GitHub, Git issues, this repository or personal development files. Use LC’s approved system and keep the contact log separate from anonymous survey responses and de-identified analysis. Do not create a link to an anonymous survey response.

| Contact-log field | Record only what is needed |
| --- | --- |
| Request reference / received date | Internal administrative reference; never a survey response identifier |
| Request route / preferred name | Self or guardian; self is Under 18 / 18 or older, guardian implies under 18. Professional contributors use self. |
| Mobile / optional email | Genuine contactable mobile on every route; optional email does not substitute |
| Contact agreement | First-contact channel, voicemail permission, safe times/instructions, notice version; later channel agreements |
| Suggested date/time and interview mode | Separate optional values; NT time unless another zone is stated; suggestions do not constitute a booking |
| Contact/access needs | Only necessary instructions and practical arrangements; no diagnosis or personal history |
| Owner / appointment | Coordinator or interviewer, confirmed time/time zone/mode and necessary private venue details |
| Status / last action / next action | New, invited, waiting for reply, waitlisted, booked, completed, cancelled, no response, unable to arrange, or round closed; action date and owner |
| Follow-up limits | Invitation/reminder sent, agreed waitlist end or reply deadline, do-not-contact instruction |
| Required participation step | Only the necessary status, such as “worker to confirm permission”; detailed assessments stay in restricted LC records |

Interview notes should record a separate interview reference, perspective, a small amount of useful context, concrete examples without identifying detail, what helped or got in the way, proposed changes and corrections made at the end. Keep any necessary link to appointment/consent records restricted within LC; never link it to the anonymous survey. Check interview permission and note retention/withdrawal arrangements with the participant using the actual LC process, not the anonymous-survey wording. Do not claim notes are irretrievable merely because the survey is anonymous.

Before absence or student placement end, the coordinator hands the backup/LC lead the current log, unresolved requests, permissions, appointment commitments, next actions and relevant record locations within the approved system. The receiving person confirms ownership. Close recruitment clearly, resolve or close outstanding invitations and waitlists, and apply LC’s retention and access arrangements; do not leave requests in a personal inbox or carry identifiable copies away after placement.

## Basis

This guide implements the team’s approved small-team consultation design. The time estimates and one-reminder approach are practical operating choices, not research sample-size rules or promises already made to participants. AIFS advises matching methods and data volume to participants’ needs, team resources and capacity to analyse the material. DVA’s consultation work provides examples of gathering family, member and service-provider perspectives without treating them as interchangeable.
