# Consultation flow and measurement contract

Updated 29 September 2026. The adult and 8–17 survey uses schema 9.0, revision 2026-09-29-rand-formal-copy. The younger-child guardian-supported form remains schema 1.3. This GitHub Pages build is a **formal-facing demonstration with no response receiver**: entered answers remain in page memory and are neither sent to Lutheran Care nor saved by the site. A real collection service, custody rules and receipt check require separate LC approval and implementation.

## Method and adaptation

The main sequence follows Laura L. Miller et al., *A New Approach for Assessing the Needs of Service Members and Their Families* (RAND, 2011), Appendix A: identify a concrete problem, connect it to the help needed, ask which resources were contacted or not contacted and what enabled or hindered access, then ask whether the need was met. [RAND publication and PDF](https://www.rand.org/pubs/monographs/MG1124.html).

The authors say the linked **problem → need → resource → outcome** approach is the contribution, and that the sample may be tailored; smaller-scale uses should collapse categories (Appendix A, p. 75). The source questionnaire was designed for US adult service members and spouses. This version uses Greater Darwin wording and local response lists, and has **not** been validated with NT Defence families. It does not import US command structure, pay grade, benefits, consent claims, telephone numbers or military-retention questions.

## Respondent route

1. **Invitation and permission.** One website-status notice before entry discloses that public submission is not open. Explain the Defence-funded plan, LC's current listening stage, the consultation's purpose and RAND adult adaptation in formal questionnaire wording. Adult, 8–17 and 7-or-younger routes keep their own permission and assistance arrangements. No question assumes that LC provides every form of support a family may need.
2. **Minimum background.** Ask ADF relationship and current local work/posting connection first, then optional broad residential area and matching suburb/locality. On the same adult page, ask optional duration of current or most recent residence in Greater Darwin and age band. An outside-area resident with a current work/posting link remains in the current-local route; residence and work/posting are different facts. The historical route is analysed separately.
3. **Problem recognition.** The adult main route uses nine grouped areas with 45 concrete locally adapted cues and an Other route. The shorter youth route uses age-appropriate topics. The checklist asks about difficulties during the applicable local recall period, including resolved difficulties; it is not a list of desired LC activities. Respondents may indicate no listed problem and still describe what has worked or might help next. Selecting a cue records a reported problem; it does **not** establish that help was needed or that a need went unmet.
4. **Choose depth.** With one or two selected problem areas, those areas proceed to detail without another choice; with more than two, the respondent chooses up to two categories. A visible Skip detailed questions action lets anyone proceed to the supplements. Other selected problems remain in the record, and a later open answer can cover them. This limit applies to the structured detail route, not to the number of problems they may report or the 100-entry technical capacity for optional supplemental accounts.
5. **Need types within each chosen category.** Refer back to its selected cues and let the person focus on the concrete cue or cues they mean. Ask what kind of help was needed; a separate explicit “no help needed” answer prevents every problem from being treated as demand. Keep all selected help types. When more than two were chosen, ask which one or two to follow through in detail.
6. **Linked pathway for each chosen category × help type.** Show both labels together, at most four detailed pathways across the response. Ask about the specific need; distinguish not seeking, seeking without receipt, seeking and receiving, and receiving without seeking. Record sources approached or used, an applicable barrier or enabling factor, whether this need was met overall, and whether any gap remains now. A contacted source does not imply help was received; received help does not imply the need was met. Do not ask contact questions after an explicit no-help-needed response.
7. **Supplementary expression.** Offer separate optional space for effective support worth keeping, future or preventive needs, and further experiences or ideas. These additions complement the linked problem route; they do not replace it or require a person to repeat the same event under every category. The previous 100-entry cap remains a technical ceiling for optional retained accounts, never a request to write 100 answers. No Berrimah activity venue is presumed.
8. **Review and finish.** Show supplied answers compactly and allow correction. The button says **Confirm and submit**. With no receiver, submission is intercepted and an accessible unavailable status states that the answer was not received; the respondent can return to their answers while the page remains open or clear them. A successful submission screen requires a real receiver acknowledgement. The independent interview-request page and free support finder receive no survey answer or join identifier.

The adult recall window is 12 months and the youth recall window three months for life connected with Greater Darwin, or the shorter period of local living/working connection. Positive and future-only answers do not acquire a past-problem or unmet-need status by inference. The child form records child expressions and guardian observations separately and does not inherit this adult questionnaire. Its first question permits a child to talk about where they live **or spend time with family**, including when they live elsewhere; the optional residence answer remains separate from that expression.

## Answer and version rules

The adult problem catalog has stable category and cue codes. Preserve the whole problem_cues selection, skip_detail_questions when explicitly used, the selected up-to-two priority_categories, and a distinct problem_details record for each category actually explored. A selected problem without detail is still a selected problem. An unselected cue is not a statement that the issue does not exist. Within a category detail record, keep optional focus_problems as a subset of that category's selected concrete cues, all help_kinds, optional other_help text and any priority_help choices. Store each detailed pathway under need_chains[need_id], keeping its source, seeking/receipt, access, met and current-gap fields tied to **that category and that need**. No chain for an unprioritised need means it was not followed through; it does not mean the need was absent. The youth route retains shorter, separately worded situation/help/asked/received/enough questions. Preserve verbatim open text. If an earlier answer changes, retain or clear dependent answers only according to the live branch rules; never silently reassign an old need's outcome to a new need.

Supplemental positive_support and future_need answers stay separate from the past-problem chain. Optional accounts have a stable ID, type and any applicable topic association; their 100-entry limit is technical capacity, not a measure of need. Do not count accounts, help types, problem cues or narrative length as separate people.

Schema 9.0 must not be pooled by matching labels with schema 8.1 issue-cued accounts, schema 8.0 free accounts or schema 7.2 topic/format responses. Keep each revision's question bank and branch snapshot. The demonstration export marks collection_mode: internal_review_no_transmission; this is a field-map example, **not** a submitted answer. Survey/contact records must remain separate. See [COLLECTION_HANDOFF.md](COLLECTION_HANDOFF.md) for platform mapping and [ANALYSIS_PROTOCOL.md](ANALYSIS_PROTOCOL.md) for denominator rules.

## Interview and pretest relationship

Use the same linked concepts in a flexible interview: what happened, what help was needed, whom the person approached or avoided and why, what worked, what remains unmet. A spoken account need not follow every survey branch verbatim. Interviews add depth where a programme decision remains unclear or writing is unsuitable. Separate cognitive/usability pretests check whether target respondents actually understand and can navigate this **local adaptation**; pretest answers are not automatically consultation findings. See [INTERVIEW_TEAM_GUIDE.md](INTERVIEW_TEAM_GUIDE.md).
